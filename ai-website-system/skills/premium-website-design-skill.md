# Skill: Premium Website Design

## What This Skill Does
Loads a set of specific design principles, standards, and guardrails that ensure every website produced by the UI Design Agent feels premium, polished, and non-generic. Prevents template-like design decisions.

## When to Use
Activate for every project. This is the baseline design quality standard for all Lumus Agency work.

## Agent That Uses It
UI Design Agent (Phase 6: Visual Direction)

### PREMIUM DESIGN PRINCIPLES FOR LUMUS AGENCY

You are designing a premium website. "Premium" does not mean expensive-looking for its own sake. It means every design decision is intentional, considered, and earned. Here is what premium actually looks like:

---

### 1. VISUAL HIERARCHY IS EVERYTHING

Premium design has clear hierarchy at every level:
- The most important thing on every page is visually dominant
- Supporting elements recede without disappearing
- Decorative elements don't compete with functional ones
- The eye knows exactly where to go next at every moment

**Anti-pattern to avoid:** Equal visual weight given to everything — where nothing stands out because everything is competing.

---

### 2. WHITE SPACE IS INTENTIONAL

- Use generous padding between sections (minimum 80px top/bottom desktop, 48px mobile)
- Give headlines room to breathe
- Don't fill every section with content — restraint is a signal of quality
- White space is not wasted space. It is the design.

**Anti-pattern:** Cramming elements together because the client wants "more content visible"

---

### 3. TYPOGRAPHY DOES THE HEAVY LIFTING

For premium brands, typography is the design:
- Use a maximum of 2 typefaces (one display, one body)
- Type scale must be clear and consistent — no improvisation
- Headlines at the large sizes signal confidence
- Line height for body text: 1.6–1.75 minimum
- Letter spacing for headlines: slightly negative (-0.01em to -0.03em for large display text)
- Never use all-caps for long text blocks

**Anti-pattern:** 3+ fonts, inconsistent sizes, tight line height on body copy

---

### 4. COLOUR IS DISCIPLINED

- Maximum 4–5 colours in the system (primary, secondary, background, text, accent)
- One dominant colour, one supporting colour, one accent used sparingly
- Background is rarely pure white (#FFFFFF) — try off-white (#FAFAF8, #F8F5F0) for warmth
- Dark mode consideration: dark backgrounds (#0D0D0D or #111111) not pure black (#000000)
- Accent colour used for CTAs and key highlights only — not scattered
- Test all colours for accessibility (contrast ratios) before finalising

**Anti-pattern:** Using 6+ colours "to make it interesting", gradients that cheapen the look, primary brand colour slapped on everything

---

### 5. IMAGERY SETS THE TONE

For local businesses and hospitality especially, photography is the most important design element:

- Real photography always beats stock photography
- If using stock, choose: editorial style, natural light, real people in real situations
- Avoid: white backgrounds, posed smiling, corporate handshakes, obvious stock
- Image cropping is a design decision — specify the focal point
- Full-width hero images must have high-quality, on-brand photography or they'll cheapen the design
- Consider: dark overlay on hero image to ensure text readability AND to create atmosphere

---

### 6. COMPONENTS MUST HAVE CHARACTER

Premium websites have components that feel designed, not defaulted:

- Buttons: choose one border-radius approach and apply it consistently (0px = sharp/editorial, 4px = clean, 100px = pill/friendly)
- Cards: define shadow depth (flat = modern/minimal; subtle shadow = classic; no shadow + border = editorial)
- Dividers: use strategically, not between every section
- Icons: choose one icon style and never mix styles
- Animations: subtle entrance animations (fade + translate) are acceptable; never add animation for animation's sake

---

### 7. MOBILE IS NOT A SMALLER DESKTOP

Mobile design must be explicitly considered:
- Hero sections on mobile: keep text short, large, and high-contrast
- Navigation: hamburger menu must be properly designed, not an afterthought
- Typography scale drops appropriately (H1 might go from 64px to 36px on mobile)
- Padding/spacing reduces proportionally (80px section padding becomes 48px)
- Image art direction: different crops for portrait screens vs landscape screens
- Tap targets: minimum 44x44px for all interactive elements

---

### 8. WHAT PREMIUM IS NOT

Avoid these anti-patterns:

- Cheap icon sets mixed randomly
- Gradient backgrounds that don't match the brand
- Drop shadows that are too strong
- Stock photos of smiling people that have nothing to do with the business
- Auto-playing video without user control
- Popups on first load
- Cookie consent banners that take over the screen
- Parallax scrolling used excessively
- Too many sections with different background colours
- Generic illustrations as a substitute for real photography
- "Hero carousel" / slideshows (almost always kill performance and conversion)

---

### DESIGN QUALITY GATE

Before submitting the visual direction, ask:

1. Could I show this to a high-paying client and be proud of it?
2. Does this design clearly belong to this specific brand (not another)?
3. Is every visual decision justified by the brand strategy?
4. Is the mobile experience explicitly designed (not just responsive)?
5. Is the hierarchy clear and intentional on every section?

If any answer is NO → revise before submitting.

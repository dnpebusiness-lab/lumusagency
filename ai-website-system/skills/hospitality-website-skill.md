# Skill: Hospitality Website

## What This Skill Does
Loads specialised knowledge for building websites for restaurants, cafés, bars, hotels, B&Bs, and hospitality businesses. Covers the specific conversion priorities, content requirements, design conventions, and copy standards for this sector.

## When to Use
Activate for: restaurants, cafés, coffee shops, bakeries, B&Bs, guesthouses, hotels, pubs, wine bars, cocktail bars, food trucks, catering businesses.

## Agent That Uses It
Copywriting Agent (Phase 4) + UI Design Agent (Phase 6)

---

## SKILL INSTRUCTIONS

### HOSPITALITY WEBSITE FUNDAMENTALS

#### What Hospitality Customers Need to Know (in priority order)

1. **Is this place for me?** (Price point, vibe, occasion match)
2. **What will I experience?** (Food, rooms, atmosphere — specifically)
3. **Is it worth it?** (Social proof, photos, reviews)
4. **Where is it and can I get there?** (Location, parking, directions)
5. **How do I book/reserve?** (Friction-free reservation)

---

#### MUST-HAVE CONTENT FOR HOSPITALITY SITES

**For Restaurants / Cafés:**
- Menu (must be on-site, not a PDF that's 2 years old)
- Opening hours (prominent and accurate)
- Location with map
- Reservation / booking method (phone, OpenTable, Resy, etc.)
- Gallery showing food AND atmosphere
- Honest price signals ("from €15" is better than hiding it)
- The story — who is the chef, what makes this place real
- Dietary options (gluten-free, vegan — increasingly important for filtering)
- Private hire / events (if applicable — separate section, separate CTA)

**For B&Bs / Hotels / Guesthouses:**
- Rooms / accommodation overview with specific descriptions
- Photography of actual rooms (not just the best room)
- Rates or rate range (remove the barrier of not knowing)
- Booking method (direct booking preferred over just linking to Booking.com)
- Local area — what's nearby, activities, attractions
- Breakfast / dining details
- Check-in/check-out times
- Cancellation policy (removes doubt)
- Accessibility information
- Guest reviews (TripAdvisor, Google, direct)

---

#### COPY STANDARDS FOR HOSPITALITY

**Use sensory language:**
Hospitality is a sensory experience. Copy must evoke it.

- INSTEAD OF: "We serve delicious food in a welcoming atmosphere."
- WRITE: "The menu changes with the season — what's on the plate in October looks nothing like August. Local lamb, wild mushrooms, and whatever arrives in that morning's delivery."

**Be specific about the experience:**
- INSTEAD OF: "A cosy B&B in the heart of Kerry."
- WRITE: "Eight rooms, each different, all with views of the estuary. Breakfast runs until 10:30. Nobody rushes you."

**Avoid the following hospitality clichés (they appear on every competitor's site):**
- "A warm and welcoming atmosphere"
- "Fresh, locally sourced ingredients"
- "A home away from home"
- "The perfect base to explore"
- "We pride ourselves on"
- "Hidden gem"
- "Award-winning" (unless it's true and recent)
- "Family-run" as a differentiator alone (say what it means in practice)

---

#### DESIGN STANDARDS FOR HOSPITALITY

**Photography is everything:**
A hospitality website without great photography cannot succeed. Flag this early if the client doesn't have professional photos.

Photo priorities:
1. Food / rooms / spaces (the product)
2. Atmosphere (people enjoying, not posing)
3. Details (textures, ingredients, craftsmanship)
4. Exterior (helps people find it and set expectations)

**Colour for hospitality:**
- Warm tones: creams, terracottas, ochres, deep greens, warm whites — for restaurants/cafés with a warm, artisanal feel
- Clean neutrals: whites, light greys, muted tones — for modern, minimalist dining
- Deep and moody: dark backgrounds, gold/copper accents — for fine dining, cocktail bars
- Fresh and light: white, sage, terracotta — for cafés, brunch spots, daytime dining

**Never use:**
- Generic blue (too corporate for hospitality)
- Clashing colours that create visual anxiety
- Hard red on white (aggressive, not welcoming)

**Typography:**
- Serif fonts often work well for premium hospitality (signal craft and tradition)
- Clean sans-serif for modern, minimal dining
- Script/handwritten fonts: use only for one element (not body copy, and only if on-brand)

---

#### CONVERSION PRIORITIES FOR HOSPITALITY

**Restaurant / Café:**
1. See the menu — or understand what kind of food without a full menu
2. Make a reservation / get the booking link
3. Get the location and directions
4. Contact for private hire / group bookings

**B&B / Hotel:**
1. See the rooms and rates
2. Book directly (avoid OTA commission)
3. Understand what's included (breakfast, parking, WiFi)
4. See the location and local area
5. Contact for specific enquiries

**Primary CTA placement:**
- Above the fold on homepage
- In the sticky navigation
- After the gallery
- At the end of every page

---

#### HOSPITALITY-SPECIFIC TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS

- **Menus:** Never upload as PDF. Implement as a page. PDFs kill SEO, don't load on mobile, and can't be updated easily.
- **Booking widget:** If using a third-party widget (OpenTable, Resy, Booking.com), test it on mobile before launch. Many are not mobile-optimised.
- **Google Business Profile:** Must be claimed, verified, and match the website NAP exactly.
- **Schema markup:** Use `Restaurant` or `BedAndBreakfast` or `Hotel` schema type (not generic `LocalBusiness`)
- **Opening hours:** Use `OpeningHoursSpecification` in schema. Update seasonally.
- **Map embed:** Embedded Google Map on Contact page is expected by users — include it.
